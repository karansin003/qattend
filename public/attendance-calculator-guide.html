1. The Fundamental Attendance Formula

University examination regulations generally require students to maintain a minimum of 75% attendance in each subject to be eligible to sit for semester examinations. The basic formula to determine your attendance percentage at any point during the semester is:

Attendance Percentage (%) = (Attended Classes / Total Classes Conducted) × 100

For example, if 40 lectures have been conducted so far and you were present for 32 of them:

  • Percentage = (32 / 40) × 100 = 80.00%
  • Since 80.00% is greater than or equal to 75%, your status is satisfactory.

2. How to Calculate Classes Needed to Reach 75%

When your attendance in a subject slips below 75%, simply attending the next class is not always enough. Every future class you attend increases both your attended count and the total class count.

Let A represent your current attended classes, T represent the total classes held so far, and k represent the number of consecutive future classes you must attend without missing any.

The mathematical inequality required to reach at least 75% is:

(A + k) / (T + k) ≥ 0.75
4 × (A + k) ≥ 3 × (T + k)
4A + 4k ≥ 3T + 3k
k ≥ 3T - 4A

Equivalently, this can be written as:

Classes Needed (k) = Math.ceil((0.75 × Total - Attended) / 0.25)

Worked Examples: Recovering Below 75%

Scenario Attended / Total Current % Calculation: 3T - 4A Classes Needed Resulting Record
Example 1 12 / 20 60.00% 3(20) - 4(12) = 60 - 48 = 12 12 classes 24 / 32 = 75.00%
Example 2 18 / 28 64.29% 3(28) - 4(18) = 84 - 72 = 12 12 classes 30 / 40 = 75.00%
Example 3 30 / 45 66.67% 3(45) - 4(30) = 135 - 120 = 15 15 classes 45 / 60 = 75.00%
Example 4 37 / 50 74.00% 3(50) - 4(37) = 150 - 148 = 2 2 classes 39 / 52 = 75.00%

3. How Many Classes Can You Miss While Staying ≥ 75%?

If your attendance is currently comfortable (e.g., 85% or 90%), you may want to know how many classes you could miss due to illness, personal events, or exam preparation without dropping below the 75% safety line.

When you miss a class, your attended count A remains constant, but the total class count increases by k.

A / (T + k) ≥ 0.75
4A ≥ 3 × (T + k) = 3T + 3k
3k ≤ 4A - 3T
k ≤ (4A - 3T) / 3

Taking the integer floor:

Margin (Skippable Classes) = Math.floor((Attended - 0.75 × Total) / 0.75)

Worked Examples: Margin & Bunkable Classes

Scenario Attended / Total Current % Calculation: (A - 0.75T) / 0.75 Can Miss Resulting % If Missed
High Buffer 18 / 20 90.00% (18 - 15) / 0.75 = 3 / 0.75 = 4 4 classes 18 / 24 = 75.00%
Moderate Buffer 24 / 30 80.00% (24 - 22.5) / 0.75 = 1.5 / 0.75 = 2 2 classes 24 / 32 = 75.00%
Tight Margin 20 / 25 80.00% (20 - 18.75) / 0.75 = 1.25 / 0.75 = 1.66 1 class 20 / 26 = 76.92% (2 misses = 74.07%)

4. How QAttend Implements These Calculations in Production

It is important to clearly distinguish between mathematical theory and what QAttend's software actually executes in its source code.

  • Exact Data from QUMS: When you connect your QUMS account, QAttend queries the authenticated student academic API. It reads the raw attended lectures and total lectures held for each subject directly from the portal.
  • Official Portal Overall Percentage: QAttend prioritizes the university's official overall percentage field (st.TotalPercentage) rather than recalculating an unweighted average, ensuring the numbers on your dashboard exactly mirror the examination cell records.
  • Zero False Security: If your percentage displays as 74.8% due to rounding, pure math might suggest 0 extra classes if rounded to 75%. QAttend explicitly enforces Math.max(1, classesNeeded) so you are never misled into believing you are safe when the official portal marks you below the required threshold.
  • Personalized Guidance: For every subject in your dashboard and Telegram summary, QAttend generates concise guidance indicating whether you are safe (with skippable count) or need attention (with consecutive classes needed).
